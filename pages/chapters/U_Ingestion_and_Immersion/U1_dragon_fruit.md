    <p>It was a chilly February evening – chilly, at least, for <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/los-angeles'>Los Angeles</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">Los Angeles</a>. I stood at the entrance of the famous Catch One nightclub, a bastion of queer of color community organizing and one of the strongholds of early AIDS activism in LA[[ Link to Page D8]]. It was a fitting venue for tonight’s event, a historic building for a historic occasion.[[ CATCH ONE]

[For more on the history of Catch One, see:]

[Fitz, C., dir. 2016. Jewel’s Catch One. Dancing Pictures.]] For the first time in the almost two years that I had been participating in and researching Los Angeles’ queer Asian American nightlife scene[[ Link to Page S]], a collaborative party was being thrown by several different community and presenting organizations[[ Link to Page S14]]. In the context of a scene that had been decimated by Covid-19 and was regrowing in patches[[ Link to Page S5]] throughout the LA metro area[[ Link to Page D9]], this was a night full of excitement and optimism – two stages, five drag performers[[ Link to Page G11]], ten DJs, an indoor night market[[ Link to Page S4]], a pole dancing showcase, and installations by local artists and sex workers, all coming together to celebrate Lunar New Year.</p>
