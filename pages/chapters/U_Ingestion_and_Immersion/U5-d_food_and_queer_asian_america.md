    <p>For Asian communities in the United States, food has long played a central role in community building, <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/identity-as-performance'>Identity as Performance</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">identity formation</a>, and economic sovereignty. This centrality is often material – such as <a class="popup-comment" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title='Asian American Food Politics' data-bs-content='For discussions of these topics, see:<br><br><a target="_blank" href="https://nyupress.org/9781479869251/eating-asian-america/">Ku, Robert Ji-Song, Martin F. Manalansan, and Anita Mannur, eds. 2013. <em>Eating Asian America: A Food Studies Reader</em>. New York: New York University Press.</a><br><br><a target="_blank" href="https://nyupress.org/9781479831364/eating-more-asian-america/">Ku, Robert Ji-Song, Martin F. Manalansan IV, and Anita Mannur, eds. 2025. <em>Eating More Asian America: A Food Studies Reader</em>. New York: New York University Press.</a>'>food’s use as a vector for political organizing, interethnic solidarity, intergenerational connection, and the articulation of an Asian American identity.<sup><b>&#8224;</b></sup></a> An explosion of Asian American food studies over the past two decades has explored these topics and more from a variety of disciplinary perspectives. Indeed, my own most fulfilling moments in <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/gaysian-nightlife-la'>Gaysian Nightlife in Los Angeles</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">LA’s Gaysian community</a> have always <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/food-in-gaysian-nightlife'>Food in Gaysian Nightlife</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">revolved around food</a>, from gathering with friends for 4 A.M. post-nightclub tofu soup to making my grandmother’s inarizushi recipe for a community potluck.</p>
    <p>Yet alongside its material importance, food has long functioned as a more abstract interpretive mechanism for Asian – <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/consumptive-constructive-of-gaysianness'>The Consumptive Construction of Gaysianness</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">and, specifically, queer Asian</a> – identity within US empire. This is not to say, <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://www.ucpress.edu/book/9780520293748/flavors-of-empire" target="_blank">as Mark Padoongpatt has cautioned against&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Padoongpatt, Mark. 2017. <em>Flavors of Empire: Food and the Making of Thai America</em>. Oakland, CA: University of California Press. 1.</span></a>, that there are inherent, culturally essential links between Asian (American) communities and food. It is, however, to acknowledge how food as a material practice and <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/ingestion-immigration-nationalism'>Ingestion, Immigration, and Nationalism</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">ingestion as a sociopolitical discourse</a> are popularly associated with Asianness in <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/greater-global-los-angeles'>Greater Global Los Angeles</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">global greater Los Angeles</a>, not just representing Asian diaspora but also discursively constructing it. Food is central <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://nyupress.org/9781479869251/eating-asian-america/" target="_blank">“to how Asian Americans have been racialized by mainstream U.S. cultural representations,”&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Cruz, Denise. 2013. “‘Love Is Not a Bowl of Quinces’: Food, Desire, and the Queer Asian Body in Monique Truong’s The Book of Salt.” In <em>Eating Asian America: A Food Studies Reader</em>, edited by Robert Ji-Song Ku, Martin F. Manalansan IV, and Anita Mannur, 356. New York: New York University Press.</span></a> in the words of Denise Cruz.</p>
    <p>Indeed, my interlocutors typically see <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/food-in-gaysian-nightlife'>Food in Gaysian Nightlife</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">the focus on food in LA’s Gaysian nightlife scene</a> as somewhat common-sensical. <a class="popup-comment" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title='燒賣' data-bs-content='Shumai (燒賣) is a type of Cantonese steamed dumpling, typically filled with either shrimp or pork.'>Miss Shu Mai (梁燒賣)<sup><b>&#8224;</b></sup></a> – a Taiwanese-American <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/drag'>Drag</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">drag queen</a> and dance teacher and one of the founders of the Gaysian drag troupe <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/send-noodz'>Send Noodz</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">Send Noodz</a> – <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)">summed up this perspective&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Interview with the author - May 24, 2024</span></a>:
        <div class="indent-quote"><p>Food in Asian culture plays a really really big role, because food is a big way we hold our culture and we share our culture, and also one of the ways where our people show love without saying the words. And I think that being able to incorporate and celebrate that and incorporate food from my culture to be able to do that, and use that in performances is a testament and how I celebrate my Asianness through my drag. And the second reason – it’s just funny! [Laughs] It’s just kinda stupid!</p></div>
    </p>
    <p>When Miss Shu Mai and Twinka Masala (to name just two examples) name themselves after food, they are <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/identity-as-performance'>Identity as Performance</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">performing a mode of identification</a> with those foods and their attendant cultural histories live on stage. <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/sexual-construction-asian-america'>The Sexual Construction of Asian America</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">The history of Asian sexuality in the US</a> is rife with such moments of identification and interpellation, from <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/history-of-gaysian-nightlife'>The History of Gaysian Nightlife in LA</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">“taste of China” nightclubs</a> in the first years of the 20th century – offering both <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://www.deepfocusproductions.com/forbidden-city-usa-book/" target="_blank">“delightful Chinese dishes” and “demure Chinese girls”&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Dong, Arthur. 2015. <em></em>. DeepFocus Productions. 16.</span></a> – to <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://archive.org/details/breathlesserotic00tsui" target="_blank">Chinese American leatherdyke Kitty Tsui’s writings in the 1990s&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Tsui, Kitty. 1996. <em>Breathless</em>. Ithaca, NY: Firebrand Books.</span></a> <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://tupress.temple.edu/books/q-a-2" target="_blank">“in which sex mingled with fermented bean curd, beef tendons, and bitter melon.”&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Sueyoshi, Amy. 2021. “Manservants to Millennials: A Brief Queer APA History.” In <em>Q&A: Voices from Queer Asian North America</em>, edited by Martin F. Manalansan IV, Alice Y. Hom, and Kale Bantigue Fajardo, 174. Philadelphia: Temple University Press.</span></a> Subject positions and identity categories within <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/gaysianness'>Gaysianness - Identity, Affect, </a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">Gaysian cultural production</a> in <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/los-angeles'>Los Angeles</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">contemporary Los Angeles</a> become enmeshed with food in ways which, through their self-consciously (and only ever partially) ironic nature, disidentify with and demand attention to this <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/consumptive-construction-of-gaysianness'>The Consumptive Construction of Gaysianness</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">ongoing history of the consumption of the queer Asian within global US empire</a>. Or, to return the mic to Miss Shu Mai: “LGBTQ stands for let’s get bánh mì tonight queen.”</p>
