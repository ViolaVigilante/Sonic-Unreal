            <footer style="padding-bottom: 1.5%;"><a class="epigraph_source" target="_blank" href="https://www.collectiveinkbooks.com/zer0-books/our-books/capitalist-realism-new-edition">Mark Fisher, <em>Capitalist Realism: Is There No Alternative?</em></a></footer>

    <p>When I tell new acquaintances that I research <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/esports'>Esports</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">esports</a>, a common follow-up to the inevitable “What’s that?” is, “Wait, you can make a living playing video games?!” Esports prompt reactions of incredulity because they seem to challenge <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://www.collectiveinkbooks.com/zer0-books/our-books/capitalist-realism-new-edition" target="_blank">what Mark Fisher has called&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Fisher, Mark. 2008. <em>Capitalist Realism: Is There No Alternative?</em> Washington, D.C.: Zero Books.</span></a> <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/social-reality'>Social Reality</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">“capitalist realism”</a> and its attendant mandate that individuals must sell their labor in order to make a living. <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/labor-in-esports'>Labor in Esports</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">Playing video games isn’t <em>labor</em></a>, my conversation partners’ shocked expressions say; playing video games is <em>fun</em>. Eventually, in our conversation, we find legible parallels in practices such as <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/are-esports-sports'>Are Esports Sports?</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">traditional sports</a> or <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/esports-and-music'>Esports & Music</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">musical performance</a> - practices where the “product” being produced is the <em>experience</em> for the audience, and practices which contain <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/playbor'>Playbor</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">both labor and pleasure</a> for different individuals in different contexts.</p>
    <p>But esports has an even more ambivalent relationship with reality than these parallel industries. All involve play, in at least some capacity, and <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/virtuality-of-play'>The Virtuality of Play</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">play itself is often understood as existing outside of social reality and systems of economic exchange</a>. Esports, however, involve an additional layer of virtuality, where performance takes place on <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://doi.org/10.1177/1555412012454222" target="_blank">what Emma Witkowski has called “the digital playing field.”&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Witkowski, Emma. 2012. “On the Digital Playing Field: How We ‘“Do Sport”’ With Networked Computer Games.” <em>Games and Culture</em> 7(5):349–74.</span></a> Many esports scholars have taken as a given that esports are fundamentally virtual and that esports’ “digital playing fields” are fundamentally worldly - leading to <a class="popup-comment" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title='Real vs. Virtual' data-bs-content='See, for example:<br><br><a target="_blank" href="https://doi.org/10.1123/jege.2024-0064">Zippan, Thorsten, and Georg Stadtmann. 2025. “B/Orders in Motion: Football Versus eFootball in Germany.” <em>Journal of Electronic Gaming and Esports</em> 3(1).</a>'>an assumed opposition between the “virtual world” of esports and the “real world” of traditional sports.<sup><b>&#8224;</b></sup></a> Dennis Hemphill has referred to esports as <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://doi.org/10.1080/00948705.2005.9714682" target="_blank">“alternative sport realities,”&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Hemphill, Dennis. 2005. “Cybersport.” <em>Journal of the Philosophy of Sport</em> 32(2):195–207.</span></a> a perspective which establishes <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/are-esports-sports'>Are Esports Sports?</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">traditional sports as the norm upon which esports are based</a> while allowing for the possibility of <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/the-multiplicity-of-realities'>The Multiplicity of Realities</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">multiple realities</a> created through different types of <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/playbor'>Playbor</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">play and labor</a>.</p>
    <p>The “alternative realities” of esports extend well beyond the computer screen, however. <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/nightlife-reality'>Nightlife & Reality</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">As with nightlife</a>, <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/esports-fandom'>(E)Sports Fandom</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">many fans turn to esports in search of a respite from real life</a> - whether <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/twitch-vignette'>Twitch Vignette</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">watching alone at home</a> or <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/attending-a-lan'>Attending a LAN</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">in a stadium with tens of thousands of others</a>. <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/sound-affect-labor'>Sound, Affect, Labor</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">Esports fandom provides a highly affectively charged experience</a>, one which breaks from <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/affective-reality'>Affective Reality</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">expected norms of how reality is meant to feel</a> and, thus, makes esports appear exterior to the real world at its most intense moments.</p>
