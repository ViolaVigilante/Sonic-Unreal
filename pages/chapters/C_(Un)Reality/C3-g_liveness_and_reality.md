    <p>Liveness - as an <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/affect-liveness'>The Affect of Liveness</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">affective orientation</a> and a <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/performativity-liveness'>The Performativity of Liveness</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">performative aesthetic</a> - is a core mechanism by which reality becomes <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/texture-liveness'>The Texture of Liveness</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">sensorially recognizable</a> as such. Many conceptions of reality are based on ideas of shared-ness, that the world you occupy and perceive is shared with others who are occupying and perceiving it in similar ways. Liveness might be <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/liveness'>Liveness</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">most basically defined</a> as the sense of shared space and time and, thus, liveness becomes a common mode through which reality is delineated.</p>
    <p><a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/liveness-television'>Liveness & Television</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">Decades of scholarship on television broadcasting</a> have demonstrated the centrality of liveness (defined for those purposes as people sharing an experience of an event in “real” time in a variety of locations, an establishing of shared temporality even at geographic distance) not only to television as a medium but to <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/social-reality'>Social Reality</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">the social orders which television inculcates</a>. Social realities are modeled through live media, <a class="popup-comment" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title='Complicating Liveness' data-bs-content='This is, of course, an oversimplification. There are some television scholars who have cautioned against the equation of liveness (in the sense of contemporaneousness) and reality, including even within some of the most canonical texts in the field.<br><br>See, for example, Feuer 1983: “To equate ‘live’ television with ‘real life’ is to ignore all those determinations standing between the ‘event’ and our perception of it - technology and institutions, to mention two” (13).<br><br><a target="_blank" href="https://archive.org/details/regardingtelevis0000unse">Feuer, Jane. 1983. “The Concept of Live Television: Ontology as Ideology.” In <em>Regarding Television: Critical Approaches, An Anthology</em>, edited by E.Ann Kaplan. Los Angeles: American Film Institute.</a>'>this scholarship tells us<sup><b>&#8224;</b></sup></a> - an expansion of Benedict Anderson’s notion of <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://www.versobooks.com/products/1126-imagined-communities" target="_blank">imagined communities&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Anderson, Benedict. 2006. <em>Imagined Communities: Reflections on the Origin and Spread of Nationalism</em>. Revised Edition. New York: Verso.</span></a> and their establishment through print media. Nick Couldry puts this succinctly: <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://doi.org/10.1080/10714420490886952" target="_blank">“Liveness - or live transmission - guarantees a potential connection to shared social realities as they are happening.”&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Couldry, Nick. 2004. “Liveness, ‘Reality’ and the Mediated Habitus from Television to the Mobile Phone.” <em>The Communication Review</em> 7:355.</span></a></p>
    <p>But beyond the realm of television, there is more to liveness - and reality - than shared temporality; physical copresence is a core component in many definitions of liveness as well. Peggy Phelan, for instance, has argued that <a class="popup-comment" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title='Peggy Phelan & Liveness' data-bs-content='<a target="_blank" href="https://www.routledge.com/Unmarked-The-Politics-of-Performance/Phelan/p/book/9780415068222">Phelan, Peggy. 1993. <em>Unmarked: The Politics of Performance.</em> New York: Routledge. 148.</a><br><br>My addition of “live” to this quote reflects Phelan’s regular shifting between the terms “performance” and “live performance” as synonymous; as she famously states, “Performance’s only life is in the present” (146).'>“[live p]erformance implicates the real through the presence of living bodies”<sup><b>&#8224;</b></sup></a> and that, in doing so, <a class="popup-citation" onmouseover="myFunction(event)" onmouseout="myFunction(event)" href="https://www.routledge.com/Unmarked-The-Politics-of-Performance/Phelan/p/book/9780415068222" target="_blank">“performance approaches the Real.”&#9873;<span class="popuptext">Phelan, Peggy. 1993. <em>Unmarked: The Politics of Performance</em>. New York: Routledge. 152.</span></a> What we see here is <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/performativity-liveness'>The Performativity of Liveness</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">the yoking of liveness and reality together through the connective mechanism of performance</a>. Reality, for Phelan, shifts from an abstractly known <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/social-reality'>Social Reality</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">social reality</a> to both an <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/liveness-and-the-body'>Liveness and the Body</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">embodied</a>, <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/material-reality'>Material Reality</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">material reality</a> and a <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/the-lacanian-real'>The Lacanian Real</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">philosophical, idealized Reality</a> (note the shift from lower-case “reality” to upper-case “Reality” in the quotes above).</p>
    <p>Regardless of how we define reality or liveness, relationships between them will appear throughout “The Sonic Unreal.” Reality - as a property of an object or experience or world - becomes sensorially known, again and again, through <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/affect-liveness'>The Affect of Liveness</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">the affect of liveness</a>. We will see this in <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/sound-affect-labor'>Sound, Affect, Labor</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">the cheering crowd at the esports tournament</a>, at <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/worlding-force-of-music'>The Worlding Force of Music</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">the virtual music festival</a>, and on <a class="popup-hyperlink" data-bs-toggle="popover" data-bs-html="true" data-bs-title="Explore Further" data-bs-content="<a href='/Sonic-Unreal/awkward-tedium-live'>The Awkward Tedium of the Live</a>" style="text-decoration: underline; font-weight: bold;">the bus to the nightclub</a>. Semantic arguments over the meanings of liveness become, through its interconnection with reality, exercises in defining reality itself.</p>
